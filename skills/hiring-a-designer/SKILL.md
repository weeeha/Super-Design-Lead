# Hiring a Designer

## Overview

The decisions that get made in hiring debriefs determine team composition for years. This skill is the discipline around interview prep, debrief structure, leveling calibration, and offer extension.

It assumes a structured loop (not 1 conversation = 1 hire). Adapt to your org's process.

## When to Use

- Preparing to write a job description
- Preparing to interview a candidate
- Running an interview debrief
- Calibrating a leveling decision
- About to extend an offer
- After a hire that worked or didn't (post-mortem)

## Phase 1 — JD and Loop Design (before posting)

### Get the level right first

Before writing the JD, get clear on the level. Pull `<vault>/people/_frameworks/design-career-ladder.md`. The level shapes everything: comp, scope expectations, who interviews them, how strict the rubric is.

If you're tempted to write "Senior or Staff" — stop. Pick one. Asymmetric hiring (open to both levels) sounds flexible but in practice biases toward the lower bar to make a hire.

### Design the loop

Standard loop for a design IC role:

| Round | Purpose | Who | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recruiter screen | Logistics, basics | Recruiter | Compensation alignment, interest level |
| Hiring manager | Career narrative, soft skills | You | Motivation, communication, level fit |
| Portfolio review | Depth of past work | You + 1 senior designer | Craft depth, decision-making, judgment |
| Take-home or onsite design exercise | Active design ability | 1-2 designers | How they think + execute |
| Cross-functional | Collaboration | PM + Eng | How they navigate constraints |
| Bar raiser (optional but valuable) | Calibration check | Senior IC outside the team | Catches halo / horns bias |

Define the **rubric per round** before any interviews. Each interviewer knows what they're testing for. Sharing rubric language across interviewers is how you avoid "I just got a good vibe."

## Phase 2 — Per-Interview Prep

For each interview:

```
## Interview Prep — <candidate> — <round> — <date>

### Level being assessed
<level>

### What this round specifically tests
<from rubric: 2-4 specific capabilities>

### Their materials reviewed
- Portfolio: <link, time spent>
- Resume: <key notes>
- Prior interview notes (if any rounds done): <summary>

### Questions I'll ask
1. <question — testing capability X>
2. <question — testing capability Y>
3. <question — testing capability Z>
4. Their question for me — preparation

### Red flags from prior rounds (if any)
- <signal to specifically probe>

### Tip-of-the-tongue context
- They asked about <X> in earlier round — be ready
- Their main concern seems to be <Y>
```

Treat interviews like 1:1s — you wouldn't wing a 1:1, don't wing an interview.

## Phase 3 — Debrief

The debrief is where bad hires get made and good hires get lost. Run it tight.

### Process

1. **Each interviewer writes their feedback BEFORE the debrief**, in the agreed-on format. Asynchronous, against the rubric. **Do not share with other interviewers before submitting.** Independence prevents anchoring.

2. **Hire / no-hire vote BEFORE discussion**, captured in writing.

3. **Then discuss.** Start with people who voted differently from the majority — their signal gets explained first.

4. **Recalibrate against rubric**, not against vibes.

5. **Decision captured in writing** with the rationale (not just outcome).

### Per-interviewer feedback template

```markdown
## Debrief — <candidate> — <round> — <interviewer>

### Vote
[ ] Strong hire  [ ] Hire  [ ] No hire  [ ] Strong no hire

### Level recommendation
<IC1 / IC2 / IC3 / IC4 / IC5 / Staff / Principal>

### Rubric scoring
| Capability | Score (1-5) | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| <capability 1> | <N> | <specific moment / artifact> |
| ... | | |

### Specific strengths observed
- ...

### Specific concerns / gaps
- ...

### What I'd want the next round to probe
- ...

### Bias check
- Was anything in my evaluation about fit, vibe, or background that wasn't job-relevant? Be honest.
```

## Phase 4 — Leveling Decision

The level you offer is irreversible without conflict later. Get it right.

For each leveling decision, write:

```
## Leveling — <candidate> — proposed level <X>

### Why this level (vs one above)
- <evidence>

### Why this level (vs one below)
- <evidence>

### Risks at this level
- Where they'll struggle
- What support gets them past it

### Comp range at this level
- <range>

### Comparison to recent hires at same level
- <comparable hire 1>: similar profile
- <comparable hire 2>: stronger in X but weaker in Y
```

If you can't articulate why-not-one-above and why-not-one-below with specific evidence, you don't have a leveling decision yet.

## Phase 5 — Offer

Once leveling is set:

1. Get the offer details (comp, equity, level, start date) before calling
2. Call them — don't email-first for the offer itself
3. Open with the role + level explicitly: *"We'd like to offer you <role> at <level>"*
4. Walk through comp without rushing
5. Ask what questions they have, what would help them say yes
6. Give a reasonable decision window (1 week typical, longer if they're balancing other offers)
7. Send the written offer immediately after

## Common Pitfalls

- **Loop-bias toward fit.** Every round optimizes for "do I like them" instead of "can they do the work." Rubric fixes this.
- **Hire/no-hire AFTER discussion.** Vote before. Discuss after. Otherwise the loudest interviewer anchors.
- **"Either level works."** No it doesn't. Pick one before you start.
- **Halo from portfolio.** A great portfolio doesn't mean they can do the work in your org. Test for it.
- **Skipping bar raiser.** Saves time short-term, costs hiring quality long-term.
- **Offering without leveling clarity.** Leads to renegotiation 6 months in.
- **"They were a vibe."** Vibes hire bad. Rubrics hire well.
- **No post-mortem on hires that didn't work.** Each miss should improve the loop.
