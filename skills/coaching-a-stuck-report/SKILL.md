# Coaching a Stuck Report

## Overview

When a report is stuck, the manager move that fails most is jumping to solutions. Solutions assume you know what's stuck. Often you don't, and they don't either. Coaching is the work of finding out.

This skill is the structure for that work: diagnose the stuckness first, then coach toward agency, not toward your answer.

## When to Use

- Performance is dropping or plateauing
- Same mistake recurring after feedback
- Low energy, withdrawn in meetings
- Career growth stalled
- Inter-personal conflict you've been hearing about
- They directly say "I'm stuck" or "I don't know what to do"

**Don't use** if the issue is corrective (e.g., misconduct, policy violation). That's `giving-feedback-to-a-report` (corrective flavor), not coaching.

**Don't use** if the report needs to be managed out. Coaching is for growth, not for documenting failure. Be honest about which situation you're in.

## The Five Flavors of Stuck

Different stucknesses need different coaching. Diagnose first.

| Flavor | Signal | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Skill gap** | Capability missing for the current scope | Training, mentorship, smaller scope to rebuild |
| **Motivation gap** | Capability present, energy absent | Career conversation, role/project realignment |
| **Confidence gap** | Capability present, doubt blocking action | Reflection, small wins, sponsorship |
| **Context gap** | Missing information / political clarity | Info-sharing, expanding their visibility |
| **Mismatch** | Wrong role / team / level for them | Honest conversation about path forward |

Skill, motivation, confidence, context, mismatch — these are not interchangeable. Treating motivation as skill gives them more training when what they needed was a role change.

## The Process

### 1. Notice and name the stuckness

Don't bring it up casually. Don't bring it up in front of others. In a 1:1, with structure:

> *"I want to step back from project stuff for a minute. I've been noticing <specific observation>. I'm not sure what's going on for you, but I want to understand. Walk me through how things have been feeling?"*

Specific observation. Not "you seem off lately."

### 2. Listen for the flavor

While they talk, listen for which flavor of stuck. Often they don't know which it is — your job is to hear it in their language.

Cues:

- "I don't know how to..." → skill gap or confidence gap (probe further)
- "I just don't care about this anymore" → motivation gap
- "I'm not sure I belong here" → confidence gap OR mismatch
- "I don't know what's expected" → context gap
- "I should be doing more but..." → motivation OR confidence (probe)
- "Nobody told me..." → context gap

Don't diagnose out loud yet. Listen until they've fully expressed it.

### 3. Reflect back to confirm

> *"Let me try to play back what I'm hearing — tell me if I'm getting it right. It sounds like <paraphrase>. Did I get that?"*

If wrong, ask them to clarify. Repeat until they say yes.

This step matters. Going to solutions without confirmation is how managers solve problems no one had.

### 4. Diagnose the flavor (silently first)

Now decide: skill, motivation, confidence, context, mismatch?

If you can't tell from one conversation, that's fine. Say so:

> *"I think there might be a few things going on here. Can we do another conversation in a few days specifically about <one piece>? I want to make sure I understand."*

Premature diagnosis is worse than slow diagnosis.

### 5. Apply the right move

**Skill gap:**
- Identify the specific skill missing
- Pair them with someone who has it
- Reduce scope until skill catches up
- Set explicit checkpoints

**Motivation gap:**
- Don't solve it in the same conversation — set up a career conversation (`quarterly-career-conversation`)
- Ask: *"What would make this energizing again?"*
- Be open to: their answer is a role change

**Confidence gap:**
- Reflect their actual capability back with specifics
- Set up small visible wins
- Sponsor them publicly (cite their work in larger forums)
- Suggest peer feedback (not just yours) to recalibrate their self-view

**Context gap:**
- Make the implicit explicit: org dynamics, who-owns-what, why-decisions-were-made
- Schedule recurring context syncs (15 min weekly) for a quarter
- Connect them to peers/stakeholders who hold the missing context

**Mismatch:**
- This is the hardest. Be honest with yourself first: is it really mismatch, or have you not given them what they need?
- If mismatch, the conversation is: *"I'm not sure this role is right for you long-term. Let's talk about what would be."*
- Options: role change within org, role shaping with their input, transition out (with support)
- Don't drag this out. Mismatch persists longer than it should because both sides avoid the conversation.

### 6. Plan and commit

End with:
- The flavor you diagnosed (share, or hold if still uncertain)
- The next step
- When you'll check in
- What support they're getting

### 7. Track

Update the report file:

```markdown
## Coaching context
- Diagnosed: <flavor> on YYYY-MM-DD
- Conversation summary: <link to 1on1-log>
- Move: <what we're doing>
- Checkpoint: YYYY-MM-DD
- Status: <progress / stalled / resolved>
```

Revisit at every 1:1 prep until resolved or re-diagnosed.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Jumping to solutions.** Diagnose first. Always.
- **Diagnosing in one conversation.** Often takes 2-3. Slow is fine.
- **Treating mismatch as skill gap.** Training a mismatched person makes them more stuck.
- **Treating motivation as skill gap.** Same. Bored people don't need more training.
- **Confusing your own discomfort with their stuckness.** They might be fine; you might just feel uncertain about them.
- **"They'll figure it out."** Sometimes. Often not. Stuck people stay stuck without intervention.
- **No follow-up.** Coaching without check-ins is venting in two directions.
- **Avoiding the mismatch conversation.** It hurts to have, but dragging it out hurts everyone more.
